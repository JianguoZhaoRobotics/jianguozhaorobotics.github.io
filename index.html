Fort Collins, CO

I am an Associate Professor of Mechanical Engineering at Colorado State University, where I direct the Adaptive Robotics Lab. My research builds robots whose bodies do part of the work: structures that change their shape, stiffness, and mode of movement so that a robot can perch, grasp, swim, roll, or reconfigure with less sensing, actuation, and computation. Examples include passive grippers that let drones perch and grasp, shape-morphing amphibious robots (Nature Communications, 2023), and tensegrity robots that link up into new shapes (Nature Machine Intelligence, 2026).

My research is supported by the National Science Foundation, including a CISE Research Initiation Initiative (CRII) award, as well as DARPA, the State of Colorado, and the U.S. Department of Transportation. Papers led by my students have been best student paper finalists at IROS and AIM.

In teaching, I develop open course materials that pair theory with Python, MuJoCo simulation, and real hardware, including an on-going free textbook Model, Simulate, Move: A Hands-On Introduction to Robotics. More on my courses is on the teaching page.

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from Michigan State University in 2015, and my M.E. and B.E. from Harbin Institute of Technology.

Prospective students: I am always looking for motivated students to join the lab. Prospective Ph.D. students interested in adaptive robotics and robot learning are encouraged to reach out and apply.
