research

Adaptive and shape-morphing robots that integrate mechanical design, control, and learning.

My group builds robots whose bodies do part of the work: structures that change their shape, stiffness, and mode of movement. The work spans four connected areas; each card below opens the area’s page on the Adaptive Robotics Lab website, with the related robots, videos, and papers. Across all four, we are increasingly interested in co-optimizing design, control, and learning, and in embodied intelligence for physical AI.

Soft Robots

Artificial muscles, soft bodies that switch between soft and rigid, and robots that change their shape and lock it in place.

Reconfigurable Robots

One body, many functions. Variable-stiffness joints, origami and tensegrity modules, and robots that link up into new shapes.

Aerial Robots

Small flying robots that perch on branches, walls, and ceilings to save energy, and grasp objects in flight.

Multimodal Robots

Small robots that move in more than one way, rolling, jumping, walking, climbing, and swimming, to cross terrain no single mode can handle.
