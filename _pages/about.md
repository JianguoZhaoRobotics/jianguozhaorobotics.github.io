I am an Associate Professor of Mechanical Engineering at Colorado State University, where I direct the [Adaptive Robotics Lab](https://adaptiverobotics.org). My research builds robots whose bodies do part of the work: structures that change their shape, stiffness, and mode of movement so that a robot can perch, grasp, swim, roll, or reconfigure with less sensing, actuation, and computation. Examples include passive grippers that let drones perch and grasp, shape-morphing amphibious robots (_Nature Communications_, 2023), and tensegrity robots that link up into new shapes (_Nature Machine Intelligence_, 2026).

My research is supported by the National Science Foundation, including a CISE Research Initiation Initiative (CRII) award, as well as DARPA, the State of Colorado, and the U.S. Department of Transportation. Papers led by my students have been best student paper finalists at IROS and AIM. 

In teaching, I develop open course materials that pair theory with Python, MuJoCo simulation, and real hardware, including an on-going free textbook [_Model, Simulate, Move: A Hands-On Introduction to Robotics_](https://jianguozhaorobotics.github.io/RoboticsBook/). More on my courses is on the [teaching]({{ '/teaching/' | relative_url }}) page.

I received my Ph.D. in Electrical Engineering from Michigan State University in 2015, and my M.E. and B.E. from Harbin Institute of Technology.
